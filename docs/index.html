Read TVL correctly.

TVL measures horizontal resolving ability, not input resolution or scanline count. Ratings use different conditions; a higher number is not an overall picture-quality score. All candidates here target standard-definition CRT use, not an assumed 480p connection.

KNOW WHAT YOU’RE COMPARING

Evidence, with the gaps left in.

Checked 17 October 2026

Verified specification

Found in a manufacturer manual or brochure. This describes the model’s original specification, not the condition or accessories of a used unit.

Uncertain or conditional

Not fully established, revision-dependent, or dependent on installed hardware. Unknown values never count as a successful desk fit.

Calculated result

Unit conversions, cabinet footprint, clearances and fit derive from the documented values and your inputs. Rounded displays do not change the internal calculation.

What needs special care

  • OEV143: RGB, component, 600 TVL, dimensions and mass are directly documented. Its 14-inch size class remains marked uncertain; no viewable diagonal is assumed.
  • PVM-2030: 560 TVL is the VTR-input rating in the cited manual edition. RGB’s 640 × 200-dot wording is retained as a historical specification, not converted into a TVL or accepted-video-mode claim. Rear connections vary by revision.
  • TM-H150CG: RGB and component need the optional IF-C01COMG card. Verify the seller includes it.
  • Ikegami TM20-16R: the service manual supports RGB, but this review did not establish external dimensions, mass, TVL, or the R variant’s component support. It remains a research candidate.
  • JVC A-series: the indexed manufacturer brochure is available in search records, but its original URL returned 404. Values are identified as documented with that retrieval limitation. Modifications are not assumed.
  • Screen size: nominal tube class and actual viewable picture are separate. Unverified viewable sizes stay blank. Cabinet drawings do not simulate picture quality.

BEFORE YOU BRING ONE HOME

Judge the unit, not just the model.

  1. Check exact model suffix and rear inputs.
  2. Test your RGB source and intended sync path.
  3. Look at a grid, solid colors, and real gameplay after warm-up.
  4. Inspect focus, convergence, geometry, burn-in and brightness.
  5. Measure the cabinet, cable protrusions and support points.
  6. Confirm included cards, adapters, service history and transport plan.
